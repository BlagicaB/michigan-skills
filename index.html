Michigan Skills · school, training and work

Michigan has so many resources. We put them all in one place.

Free college classes in high school. A free 13th year. Free community college. Apprenticeships that pay you from day one. Money for employers who train. It all exists in this state, but it's spread across dozens of agencies, 56 school districts, 31 colleges and hundreds of training programs, so it's hard to find. We gathered it here, in one fell swoop, in plain English.

Five things most Michigan families never hear about

  1. Two free online classes every academic term, starting in grade 6. Not two total. Two per academic term (semester or trimester), paid for by your district. How it works
  2. A free 13th year of high school that ends with an associate degree. It's called Early Middle College, and you have to plan for it by grade 10. How it works
  3. CTE can replace Algebra II and a world language toward graduation. Welding in the morning and AP Chemistry in the afternoon is allowed. How it works
  4. Community college is free for every graduate since 2023 and, as of this year, for adults 21 and older without a degree. How it works
  5. Employers can be reimbursed for training new hires, often half their wages during training, through your local Michigan Works! office. How it works

Quick answers

What free training and education does Michigan offer?

A lot. Students get free online courses from grade 6, college classes the district pays for from grade 9 and a possible free 13th year that ends in an associate degree. Graduates since 2023 and adults 21 and older can get free in-district community college, and registered apprenticeships pay you while you learn.

Source: MI Student Aid

Who is Michigan Skills for?

Students, parents, adults changing careers, employers and the teachers, counselors and job center staff who help them. Each group has its own plain-English guide, and the You Are Here planner builds a personal plan by county and grade.

Source: About Michigan Skills

Is Michigan Skills a government website?

No. Michigan Skills is a free, independent guide. It doesn't run any of the programs it describes, and every fact links to the agency, law or program that publishes it, so you can confirm details before you apply.

Source: About Michigan Skills

Where do I start if I don't know what my child wants to do?

Start with what they enjoy, not a career. The You Are Here planner asks what they love doing and suggests camps, robotics teams and programs near you to try first, plus a grade-by-grade checklist. Not knowing yet is normal.

Source: Michigan Skills planner
