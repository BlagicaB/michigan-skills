For employers and apprenticeship sponsors

Can't find skilled workers? Train them, and get help paying for it.

Michigan and the federal government will pay part of what it costs to train new hires and upskill the people you have. Most of it runs through your local Michigan Works! agency, which is free to talk to. Here's what's available, what it pays and the honest fine print.

Money to train

Federal · through Michigan Works!

On-the-job training (OJT) wage reimbursement

You hire someone who qualifies for WIOA (often a laid-off worker, veteran or low-income job seeker) and train them on the job. Michigan Works! reimburses part of their wages during training, typically up to 50%, and up to 75% where the local board allows. Caps are set locally. Oakland County, for example, reimburses up to half of wages up to $5,000 per trainee. Registered apprentices can qualify.

20 CFR 680.730 · Oakland County Michigan Works!, OJT

State · competitive

Going PRO Talent Fund

Grants for training that leads to an industry-recognized credential, for new hires or current employees, including registered apprentices. The average award since 2014 is about $41,000, and the average cost per trainee is about $1,600. The next round opens in early 2027. Apply through your Michigan Works! agency.

Fine print: the fund was cut sharply, to $14.5 million for 2026-27, and a September 2026 state audit examined how awards are selected and monitored. Expect more competition and stricter documentation.

LEO, Going PRO Talent Fund · LEO, July 14, 2026 · Office of the Auditor General

State · through community colleges

Michigan New Jobs Training Program

If you are creating new jobs in Michigan, a community college can train those new hires at no upfront cost to you. It's repaid from the state income tax withheld from the new employees' wages. Any industry, any size. New jobs must pay at least the county's ALICE wage. Start with your local community college's workforce office.

Michigan Community College Association, MNJTP

Federal · apprenticeship

State Apprenticeship Expansion funds

Federal money passed through LEO that partly reimburses training costs for registered apprenticeship sponsors, plus limited support services. In April 2026, $4 million went to 13 partners for 606 apprentices. Funds go out in rounds through intermediaries like Michigan Works! agencies and industry groups, so ask your agency which round is open.

LEO, April 20, 2026

On hold

Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC)

A federal tax credit of 25% to 40% of first-year wages for hiring from certain groups (veterans, people on public assistance and others). It lapsed on December 31, 2025 and needs Congress to renew it. Michigan is still accepting and holding applications, and past renewals have usually been retroactive. File anyway, within the normal 28-day window, so you're covered if it comes back.

LEO, WOTC

Start a registered apprenticeship

Registered apprenticeship is the most durable fix for a skills gap: about 94% of apprentices stay with the employer that trained them. You don't need to build it alone.

  1. Step 1

    Tell the state you're interested

    Fill out LEO's Employer Interest Form. Pick the jobs you need and the skills that matter.

  2. Step 2

    Join an existing program or start your own

    The easiest path is joining a group sponsor (an industry association, Michigan Works! agency or college that already holds a registered program). You supply the job and the mentor. They handle the paperwork and classes.

  3. Step 3

    Line up classes and mentors

    Related instruction usually comes from a community college or training center. Plan for at least 144 classroom hours a year and about 2,000 job hours a year.

  4. Step 4

    Register and get reimbursed

    Programs are registered with the U.S. Department of Labor's Michigan office. Once registered, your apprentices may qualify for OJT, Going PRO and expansion funds, and veterans can use the GI Bill.

LEO apprenticeship for employers

Sources: LEO, Employers · U.S. DOL, State apprenticeship offices · 29 CFR 29.5

Build your pipeline from the high school up

Every state-approved CTE program in Michigan is required to have an advisory committee, with a majority of members from the related industry, that meets at least twice a year. They need you. It's the cheapest recruiting you will ever do.

  • Sit on an advisory committee at your ISD tech center. You shape what students learn and meet them before anyone else does.
  • Host work-based learning. Students who have finished half a CTE program and passed safety training can work in your shop. Unpaid placements need liability insurance; paid ones need workers' comp.
  • Sponsor a youth apprentice (16+), including inside an Early Middle College.
  • Show up at MiCareerQuest, the hands-on career expos regional Michigan Works! agencies run for thousands of high schoolers each year.
  • Hire CTE completers straight into apprenticeships. Several unions already offer direct entry.

Find your ISD tech center

Sources: MDE, CTE Administrator Manual, Section 5 · MDE, Work-Based Learning

Hiring under 18: the rule just changed

As of October 2, 2026, youth work permits come only from the state, through LEO's Youth Employment Permit Portal. Schools no longer issue them. Both the employer and the minor have to register. Minors already working for you don't need to re-register until they change employers or work locations, or turn 16.

Minors can't do hazardous work, with one important exception: 16 and 17 year olds who are registered apprentices or students in a state-approved CTE program can do some hazardous tasks under a written training agreement, close supervision and school safety instruction.

Sources: LEO, Youth Employment Standards Act · LEO, Youth Employment Permit Portal · MCL 409.104a

Who does what in Michigan talent

Five public players, and it helps to know which door to knock on. The short version: MEDC brings companies here, LEO and Michigan Works! train and place workers, MiLEAP pays for school after high school and MDE runs high school CTE.

MEDC programs worth knowing

  • Talent Action Team: a concierge service that builds a training pipeline around a key employer's priority jobs, with nearby colleges and schools.
  • The Michigander Scholars: $5,000 to $10,000 for engineering and tech college students who intern or take a full-time job with a participating employer and stay in Michigan for a year. Employers in EV, mobility, semiconductors and aerospace and defense can join.
  • Michigan New Jobs Training Program: promoted by MEDC, run by community colleges (see above).

Common questions

How can Michigan employers get paid to train workers?

Three main ways. On-the-job training through Michigan Works! reimburses part of a qualifying new hire's wages during training, typically up to 50%. The state's Going PRO Talent Fund awards training grants, with the next round in early 2027. And the Michigan New Jobs Training Program lets community colleges train new hires at no upfront cost.

Source: 20 CFR 680.730

Do I need a work permit to hire a minor in Michigan?

Yes. Since Oct. 2, 2026, youth work permits come only from the state's Youth Employment Permit Portal, not from schools, and both the employer and the minor register. Minors already working for you don't need to re-register until they change employers or work locations, or turn 16.

Source: Michigan LEO
