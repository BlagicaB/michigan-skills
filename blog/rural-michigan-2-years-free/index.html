Rural Michigan Deserves 2 Years Free Too

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

Michigan will pay for two years of community college tuition if you qualify. I think that's one of the best things this state does. But when I went through the rules, one gap kept bothering me.

The money only works at a community or tribal college. If you live somewhere that doesn't have one close by, but there's a public university down the road that offers the same two-year degree, the program doesn't follow you there.

Think about Big Rapids. Ferris State University has been granting associate degrees and certificates since the early 1950s. It's also about a two-hour round trip from the nearest community college. A student there can have a two-year program in town and still not be able to use the state's free tuition on it.

I'm not the only one saying this

According to Bridge Michigan's October 2026 reporting, Ferris State, Northern Michigan, Lake Superior State and Michigan Tech are asking lawmakers to extend the Community College Guarantee to students earning two-year degrees at their schools. Ferris's president calls these areas "community college deserts." State Rep. Tom Kunse, who represents the county where Ferris sits, told Bridge the expansion makes sense, though he hasn't introduced legislation. I couldn't find a bill yet.

The idea has been around. The state's 2023 Growing Michigan Together report recommended two years of tuition at a community college or public university. So this needs more voices, and parents and students have the most convincing ones.

Who gets left out

  • About 80% of Michigan residents live in a community college district. The rest don't, and they are concentrated in rural places.
  • Reporting and research point to most of the Upper Peninsula, the Thumb and parts of the northern Lower Peninsula as the areas with the thinnest coverage.
  • Students outside a district can still use the programs at a community college, but they only get up to the in-district rate and pay the difference, on top of the drive.

What it costs a family today

Here's one real comparison from this school year. At Montcalm Community College, 30 hours costs about $5,100 in-district and $8,580 out-of-district with fees, by our math from its published rates. At Ferris, 28 credits runs $14,952 in tuition alone.

Some help exists. The Michigan Achievement Scholarship pays up to $5,500 a year at public universities for students who meet its income test, and Northern Michigan's Tuition Advantage makes tuition free for students with both that scholarship and a Pell Grant. But community college students face no income test at all. Where you happen to live shouldn't change that.

The fair objection

This wouldn't be free for the state. Universities cost about three times what community colleges do, and Bridge reports the bill could reach $18 million to $20 million a year if the state paid full university tuition for those students. The head of the Michigan Community College Association called that very expensive.

That's a real concern, and I don't think it ends the conversation. One compromise raised in that reporting is discounted tuition for associate students at those universities. Another way to do it: pay the same in-district amount the state would pay at a community college, limit it to places without one nearby and have the universities cover the rest. Rural families shouldn't get nothing because the full version costs more.

Other states already do this

  • Tennessee Promise works at community colleges, technical colleges and other eligible schools offering an associate degree.
  • New Mexico's Opportunity Scholarship covers tuition at any public college or university.
  • Kentucky's Work Ready scholarship includes approved programs at some public universities.

What it would take

This is a job for the Legislature. Reconnect limits eligible schools to public community colleges in state law, so that law would have to change. The Guarantee is written into Section 248 of the School Aid Act, which lawmakers amend through the education budget.

What you can do

  • Contact your state representative and state senator. Lawmakers respond to constituents with a specific story.
  • Tell them how far you are from a community college, what's close instead and what it would change for your family.
  • If you're at a school or college in one of these areas, share this with your families.
  • Email me your story. I want to collect what this looks like in real towns.

A message you can copy

"I live in [town]. The nearest community college is [distance] away, but [university] is close and offers two-year degrees. Michigan's Community College Guarantee and Reconnect programs can't be used there. Please support extending them to two-year programs at public universities in areas without a nearby community college."
