Michigan Work Permits for Teens Changed in October. Here's How It Works Now.

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

If your teen is about to get a job, the process you remember is gone. Since October 2, 2026, Michigan schools no longer issue work permits. Everything runs through the state's online Youth Employment Permit Portal.

How to get a work permit now

  • Create an individual MiLogin account. A parent can register for the minor.
  • In MiLogin, search for "Youth Employment Permit Portal" and request access. It's free, and approval usually takes minutes.
  • The teen fills in contact details, parent, school and employer.
  • The employer enters job details and pay. Employers must be registered with the state before hiring minors.
  • A parent reviews it when required, then the state approves it.

The state has five days to decide. If it hasn't sent a notice by then, work may begin the next day.

Do old permits still count?

Yes. A valid permit issued by a school is honored. Your teen needs a new one through the portal when changing employers.

Who needs one

Anyone under 18, and the general minimum age is 14, with exceptions for jobs like caddying and refereeing. Some work is exempt: chores in a private home, a business owned and run by a parent who works there and farm work that meets state standards. If you aren't sure about something like babysitting, ask the state's Wage and Hour Division.

Hours and pay did not change

  • Ages 16 and 17 can work up to 24 hours a week when school is in session, 10 hours in a day and not past 10:30 p.m. (11:30 p.m. on Fridays, Saturdays and vacations).
  • Workers under 18 can be paid 85% of minimum wage.

This could change again

House Bill 5727, which would repeal the permit requirement and have employers get proof of age instead, passed the House on July 1, 2026 and went to a Senate committee. It isn't law. Permits still apply.
