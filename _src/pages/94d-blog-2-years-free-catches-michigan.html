2 Years Free in Michigan: The Catches Nobody Mentions

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

You may have seen the ads: two years of college, free, in Michigan. It's true, and I'm glad the word is getting out. But "free" has edges, and it's better to find them now than on your first tuition bill.

The seven catches

1. It's about where you live

The programs pay the in-district rate. If the college is outside your district, you pay the difference. What to do: ask the college, in writing if you can, whether you count as in-district.

2. Books aren't included

Neither program pays for textbooks or supplies. What to do: ask for an estimate per semester and ask whether the college rents books or uses free course materials.

3. The Guarantee has a clock

You have 15 months after high school to enroll. What to do: if your child is taking a gap year, put the deadline on the calendar now.

4. The Guarantee means full time

Your college decides how many credits that is. What to do: if you need to work a lot of hours, compare this with Reconnect once you're 21. Here's how the two compare.

5. You have to keep filing the FAFSA

The Guarantee requires it every year. What to do: set a reminder for October, when the form usually opens.

6. Reconnect has rules to keep it

You need at least 12 credits an academic year and either a 2.0 GPA or satisfactory progress as your college defines it. Application and graduation fees aren't covered either. What to do: talk to a Reconnect navigator before you drop a class.

7. It stops at community and tribal colleges

A two-year degree at a public university doesn't qualify, even where that university is the only option nearby. I think that's unfair to rural families, and people are trying to change it.

None of this means skip it

Every one of these is manageable if you know about it ahead of time. That's my whole reason for building this site. Start with the 2 years free guide.
