How to become a CNA in Michigan

To become a CNA in Michigan, you finish a state-approved 75-hour nurse aide training program and pass the state competency exam, then get listed on Michigan's Nurse Aide Registry. There is no minimum age for training and testing. If a Medicaid-certified nursing home hires you within 12 months of your exam, it must pay you back for training and testing. CNAs earn a median of $19.03 an hour.

Last checked: October 2026. Pay is the Michigan median from the federal government (May 2025).

Ways to get trained, cheap or free

In high school

Many career tech center health science programs include CNA training, and there's no minimum age to train and test. CTE guide

Get it paid for

A Medicaid-certified nursing facility must reimburse your training and testing if it hires you within 12 months of testing. Hospitals and home care employers don't have to. Some nursing homes train new hires for free.

Community college or adult ed

Short CNA courses are common. Ask Michigan Works! whether it can cover the cost.

The next steps

CNA is a common first step into nursing. Michigan LPNs earn a median of $31.47 an hour and RNs $45.34.

Short training for this job may qualify for Workforce Pell once Michigan approves specific programs, so ask the provider.
