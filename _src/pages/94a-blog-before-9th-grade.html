What Michigan Parents Should Ask Before 9th Grade

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

My son is in 8th grade. When he started asking about the different paths in our district, I went looking for answers and ended up surrounded by Post-it notes and lists.

I work in digital strategy, so finding and organizing information online is what I do for a living. It still took me websites, notes, conversations with leaders at local community colleges and questions on social media to get anything close to the full picture. Social media is where I learned how popular Early Middle College has become.

If it was that hard for me, I knew it was hard for a lot of families. Then I learned something about my own daughter that still bothers me.

The Italian class my daughter could have kept taking

My daughter took Italian in 8th grade over at the high school. The high school she went on to attend didn't offer it, and we could not get her to the other high school for first hour. So Italian ended. She loves Spanish, but Italian was her first interest.

I only recently learned that she could have kept going online.

Michigan students in grades 6 through 12 can take up to two online courses per academic term, paid for by the district. Districts can turn down a request for certain reasons, so you do have to ask. But nobody can ask about something they've never heard of.

I was disappointed. Through a benefit that already existed, my daughter could have continued something she loved. What was missing was the information.

Why families don't hear about these options

It isn't always the school

Our counselors are great. This isn't a story about a school failing my family. Counselors are answering a huge range of needs, and every program has its own rules, deadlines and website.

Most parents stay quiet about community college

I believe part of the problem is us. Most parents are quiet about community college as a potential path, so it isn't discussed much. It depends on the community, but a lot of it is status. When parents don't talk about a path, what they know about it never gets passed along.

Saving money is changing the conversation

In this economy, community college and saving money is almost seen as a flex. I think that's a good thing. Michigan will pay for two years of community college tuition if you qualify, and the state has put the savings at up to $4,800 a year. That's worth talking about out loud.

Start with an honest talk at home

Before you ask a counselor anything, talk with your child. Let them know there is no pressure and no assumption that they have to fit a certain path.

That level of trust matters more than any program on a list. A kid who thinks they already know the answer you want will give you that answer. You want to hear what they're really curious about, even if it surprises you.

Ask for the full map before 9th grade

Once you've had that talk, ask your school or counselor to lay out every path available to your child before 9th grade starts. The timing matters because some doors open early. Some early colleges take applications in the winter of 8th grade.

Questions about classes

  • What can my child take online that this school doesn't offer?
  • How do we request an online class and what's the deadline?
  • If a class is offered at another building, how would my child get there?

That last question is the one that ended Italian for us.

Questions about college credit

  • Can my child take college classes during high school? Dual enrollment can start as early as 9th grade with a qualifying test score.
  • Which colleges does the district work with and what are the deadlines?
  • Is there an Early Middle College my child can reach, whether it's run by the district, the county or a nearby school?

Questions about careers and the trades

  • Where is career and technical education taught and is there a bus?
  • How would those classes fit with the rest of my child's schedule?
  • Are there apprenticeships or paid work placements for students here?

School districts need to set the bar

Parents shouldn't need a wall of Post-it notes to find out what their kids can do. Right now, how much a family hears depends too much on the school district they happen to live in.

The district needs to set the bar. Every family should see the full set of options before 9th grade, in plain English, while there's still time to use them.

Why I built Michigan Skills

Once I had all of those notes, I decided to put everything in one place. Michigan Skills brings together the college credit options, career programs, trade paths, apprenticeships and free community college programs I researched. It's free and independent, and every fact links back to its source.

If you know a program I missed or see something that needs fixing, please reach out. This works best when parents, counselors and program staff all add what they know.

Start with the talk at home. Then go ask for the map.
