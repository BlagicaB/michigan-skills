2 years free community college in Michigan: who gets it and the catches

Michigan will pay for two years of community college tuition if you qualify through one of two state programs. The Community College Guarantee covers people who finished high school in 2023 or later, and Michigan Reconnect covers adults 21 and older without a college degree. Both pay in-district tuition and mandatory fees. Neither pays for books, and if the college is outside your district you get a discount instead of free tuition.

Why people say "two years": a full-time associate degree normally takes two years, and the state has put the savings at up to $4,800 a year for an in-district student. Both programs also cover skill certificates, including many trades programs, which can take far less time.

You may have seen flyers or ads for 2YearsFree.com. That campaign points to these same two state programs. Michigan Skills is independent and isn't connected to it.

What are the catches?

The programs are real and they're a great deal. "Free" just has edges, and these are the ones that surprise people.

  • Where you live decides the price. Free tuition applies at the college whose taxing district you live in. Go outside it and you get a discount, then pay the rest. Reconnect says plainly that you owe the difference. Before you enroll, ask the financial aid office: "Am I in-district?"
  • Tuition isn't the whole bill. Books, supplies and living costs aren't covered. Reconnect also leaves out application and graduation fees and fees not charged to every student, like parking.
  • The Guarantee has a clock. You have 15 months after high school to enroll full time. Miss it and your next free option is usually Reconnect at 21.
  • You have to file the FAFSA. Both programs pay what's left after grants like Pell, and the Guarantee needs a new FAFSA every year.
  • You have to keep it. Reconnect requires at least 12 credits an academic year and either a 2.0 GPA or satisfactory progress as your college defines it.
  • A degree can disqualify you. Reconnect is only for adults without an associate or bachelor's degree.

How do you get it?

  1. File the FAFSA. Do this first. It's required either way.
  2. If you're 21 or older, fill out the Reconnect application. The state says it takes about 5 minutes. Recent graduates don't need a separate application for the Guarantee.
  3. Apply to your community college. Find yours and confirm you're in-district.
  4. Ask for an estimate of what you'll still owe so books and fees don't catch you off guard.

Live in a Promise Zone, Detroit or Kalamazoo? A local scholarship may cover more.

What can you do with two free years?

Go straight to work

Earn a certificate in a field that's hiring. See pay and training for electricians, HVAC technicians, welders, lineworkers and nurse aides.

Transfer for a bachelor's

Finish two years, then transfer. The Michigan Achievement Scholarship can help pay at a university if you qualify.

Still in high school? You may be able to start earlier and pay nothing through dual enrollment or Early Middle College.
