Community College Guarantee or Reconnect: Which One Fits You?

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · October 27, 2026 · Updated November 8, 2026

Michigan has two free community college programs, and people mix them up constantly. Usually one question sorts it out: how old are you, and when did you finish high school?

You probably want the Guarantee if

  • You finished high school or an equivalency in 2023 or later.
  • You can enroll within 15 months of graduating.
  • You can go full time.

There's no income limit, you can renew for up to three years and Pell-eligible students get $1,000 more. You don't fill out a separate application. The FAFSA unlocks it.

You probably want Reconnect if

  • You're 21 or older.
  • You don't have an associate or bachelor's degree.
  • You need to go part time. Twelve credits an academic year is enough.

You get up to four years to finish, and it covers Pell-eligible skill certificates, including many trades programs. You do have to apply separately and file the FAFSA.

If you're 21 to 24, don't wait

The age dropped from 25 to 21 in the 2026-27 budget. The state describes the funding as ongoing, but Bridge Michigan reports advocates say the expansion is only for one year. Those two don't match, so I'd apply while you know you qualify.

The gap to watch

If you're 19 or 20 and missed the 15-month window, neither program may fit right now. Call your college's financial aid office and Michigan Works!, which can fund some training. Then put your 21st birthday on the calendar.

What's the same

Both pay in-district tuition and mandatory fees after other grants. Neither pays for books. Both work at Michigan's public community colleges and tribal colleges, and both leave you with the difference if you're out-of-district.
