How to become an electrician in Michigan

To become an electrician in Michigan, you register as an electrical apprentice, work 8,000 hours over at least 4 years under a licensed electrician while taking classes, then pass the state journeyman exam at age 20 or older. Union apprenticeships pay you the whole time and the training is free. Michigan electricians earn a median of $36.67 an hour, with about 2,450 openings a year.

Last checked: October 2026. Pay is the Michigan median from the federal government (May 2025).

Ways to get trained, cheap or free

In high school

Take electrical or construction trades at your county career tech center. Some union programs give CTE grads a head start. CTE guide

Union apprenticeship

IBEW and NECA run free training centers. You earn while you learn, starting around 45% of journeyman pay and rising to 80% (West Michigan example). Detroit, IBEW 58 · West Michigan, IBEW 275 · Lansing, IBEW 665 · Ann Arbor, IBEW 252 · Kalamazoo, IBEW 131 · U.P., IBEW 906. In Flint and Saginaw, ask your Michigan Works! office for the local training center.

Non-union employers

Many contractors register their own apprentices. Ask Michigan Works! about on-the-job training openings.

Community college

Electrical certificates count toward your related classes. Free for many adults through Reconnect or recent grads through the Guarantee.

Short training for this job may qualify for Workforce Pell once Michigan approves specific programs, so ask the provider.
