For teens 16 and 17

Get paid at 16 for learning a career

At 16 you can earn money in jobs that actually lead somewhere: a paid youth apprenticeship, a summer jobs program, a paid co-op through your school's career tech program, or a starter job like nurse aide or auto service helper. Michigan's minimum wage is $13.73 an hour, and employers can pay workers under 18 at least $11.67.

Last checked: October 2026.

Five ways to earn while you learn

1. A paid youth apprenticeship

What: a real job with training and a national credential at the end. Paid from day one, with raises as you learn. More than 8,400 Michigan youth have done one.

Next step: ask your CTE teacher or call Michigan Works! at 1-800-285-9675.

2. A summer jobs program

What: paid summer work set up for you. In Detroit, Grow Detroit's Young Talent pays $12.50 to $15 an hour for up to 120 hours in July and August. Sign-up was March to May in 2026.

Next step: outside Detroit, ask Michigan Works! about youth summer jobs. GDYT

3. A paid co-op through CTE

What: if you're in a career tech program, your school can place you with a real employer, sometimes paid. A signed training plan replaces the work permit.

Next step: ask your CTE teacher about work-based learning. What CTE is

4. Become a nurse aide (CNA)

What: 75 hours of training, a state test, then jobs paying a median of $19.03 an hour. There's no minimum age to train, and nursing homes that hire you may have to pay you back for training.

Next step: ask if your tech center's health program includes it. CNA guide

5. Start in auto

What: auto techs in Michigan earn a median of $26.02 an hour, and starters earn about $17. In high school you can earn ASE Entry-Level certifications. After, carmakers run paid college programs: Ford ASSET at Henry Ford College, GM ASEP at Macomb and Delta, Mopar CAP at Mott.

Next step: find automotive CTE at your county tech center.

Bonus: heavy equipment

What: Operating Engineers Local 324 has a direct-entry apprenticeship for CTE grads who took construction, line worker or heavy equipment programs.

Next step: take one of those programs junior or senior year. OE 324

The rules for working at 16 and 17

  • Hours during school: up to 24 a week, no more than 10 in a day and not past 10:30 p.m. (11:30 p.m. on Fridays, Saturdays and vacations).
  • Summer: up to 48 hours a week.
  • Breaks: 30 minutes after 5 hours in a row.
  • Work permits moved online on Oct. 2, 2026. Your school doesn't issue them anymore. Your employer registers with the state, and you fill out the permit online through MiLogin.
  • Pay: at least $11.67 an hour under 18 ($13.73 for adults), going up Jan. 1, 2027. New hires under 20 can be paid a lower training wage for the first 90 days.
