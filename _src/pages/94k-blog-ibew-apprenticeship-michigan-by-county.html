Which IBEW Local Covers My County and When Does It Open?

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

If you want to become a union electrician in Michigan, the first problem is figuring out which program is yours. Each IBEW local has its own territory, its own dates and sometimes its own residency rule. Here's what I could confirm as of November 2026.

Michigan programs by area

County lines come from the IBEW's Michigan jurisdiction map, which is dated 2018, along with each program's own site.

Detroit area: Local 58

Wayne, Oakland, Macomb, St. Clair, Sanilac, Huron and part of Livingston. Currently closed, with 2027 dates due in spring 2027. $50 fee.

Ann Arbor and Jackson: Local 252

Washtenaw, Jackson and some Livingston and Ingham townships. Applications are in person by appointment on the third Monday, with Nov. 16 and Dec. 21 next. You must have lived in the area 12 months.

West Michigan: Local 275

Mason, Oceana, Newaygo, Muskegon, Mecosta, Montcalm, Kent, Ottawa, Ionia and part of Isabella. Usually opens quarterly. $25 fee. Proof of residency required.

Lansing: Local 665

Clinton and most of Eaton and Ingham, plus part of Livingston. Opens the third Monday, but inside wireman applications pause October through December and resume in January 2027.

Kalamazoo: Local 131

Allegan, Van Buren, Kalamazoo and St. Joseph. Takes applications year-round. $50 fee.

Battle Creek: Local 445

Calhoun, Branch, the east half of Barry and the west quarter of Eaton. No residency requirement. Accepts requests all year, with testing February to April.

Flint and Saginaw: Locals 948 and 557

Genesee, Lapeer and Shiawassee (948). Saginaw, Gratiot and Tuscola, plus parts of neighboring counties (557). 2027 applications close Jan. 31, 2027. $25 fee. You need two semesters of algebra with a C or better.

Bay City and the northeast: Local 692

Northeast Michigan, including Bay, Midland, Arenac, Iosco, Alpena and Presque Isle. Online on the first and third Wednesday, with Nov. 18, Dec. 2 and Dec. 16 next.

Traverse City and the northwest: Local 498

Northwest Michigan, including Grand Traverse, Emmet, Charlevoix, Antrim, Leelanau, Benzie, Manistee and Wexford. You must live in the area. Call to confirm the current application day, because the posted schedule may be old.

Upper Peninsula: Local 906

The 906 JATC says it covers every county in the U.P. Request an application from the program. $25 fee, which can be waived by letter.

Border counties

Berrien and Cass fall under Local 153 in South Bend. Hillsdale, Lenawee and Monroe fall under Local 8 in Toledo.

If your county is split

Barry, Eaton, Ingham, Livingston, Isabella, Clare, Roscommon, Crawford, Otsego and Cheboygan are divided between locals. Some programs require you to live in their area, so your township can decide where you may apply. Call the nearest training center and ask which local covers your address before you pay a fee.

What you'll need

  • Age 17 or 18, depending on the program
  • A diploma or GED, with an official transcript
  • Usually a year of high school algebra, often with a C or better
  • Usually a driver's license

The test

Most programs use the national aptitude test in algebra and reading. It's about 2.5 hours, and you can retake it after 3 months. Then comes an interview and a ranked list that lasts one to two years. Practice the algebra first. A better score can move you up the list.

Non-union options

Associated Builders and Contractors chapters also run electrical training: Southeastern Michigan, Greater Michigan and Western Michigan. Ask whether you need an employer first.
