Early Middle College or Dual Enrollment: Which Is Right for My Kid?

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

I learned how popular Early Middle College has become by asking questions on social media. Nobody had sat me down and explained it. If you're in the same spot, here's the difference in plain English.

The short version

Dual enrollment is a few college classes. Early Middle College is a five-year plan.

Dual enrollment

Students who qualify take college courses their high school doesn't offer, and the state funds up to 10 courses, with limits by grade. It can start in 9th grade with a qualifying test score. Your child graduates on time. Full guide

Early Middle College

This is a state-approved five-year program. Your child stays in high school one extra year and finishes with a diploma plus at least one of these: 60 transferable credits, an associate degree, a professional certification, a technical certificate or a registered apprenticeship.

How to think about it

Dual enrollment tends to fit a student who wants to test college or get ahead in a few subjects. Early Middle College tends to fit one who already has a direction and would really use that fifth year. Before you pick either, have the honest talk at home. Your child needs to know there's no assumed path.

Trade-offs to ask about

  • Sports: the state's Early Middle College manual says there are no high school athletics in the 13th year.
  • The social side: friends graduate a year ahead. Talk about how that feels.
  • NCAA plans: if your child may play Division I or II sports, check how dual enrollment courses are recorded. They have to show a grade and count in the high school GPA.
  • Transfer: ask whether credits apply to the college your child might attend. Don't assume.
  • Timing: some programs start early. Henry Ford Early College takes Wayne County students entering 9th grade, with applications in the winter of 8th grade.

Does either one use up free community college?

No. Students can still use the Community College Guarantee after graduating, as long as they enroll within 15 months and meet the other rules. Early Middle College and dual enrollment semesters don't count as starting it.

What to ask your school

  • Do we have an Early Middle College, or can my child reach one through the county?
  • Which colleges do we dual enroll with and what are the deadlines?
  • When would we have to decide?
