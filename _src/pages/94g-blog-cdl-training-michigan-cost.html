CDL Training in Michigan: Real Costs and Who Can Pay for It

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

Most pages about CDL training are trying to sell you a school. Here's what it costs at public colleges and what to check before you hand anyone money.

What it costs

Class A programs at Michigan community colleges run about $4,500 to $5,200:

  • Alpena Community College: $4,500
  • Gogebic Community College: $5,008
  • Kirtland Community College: $5,200
  • Lake Michigan College: $5,200

Short CDL courses often can't use regular college financial aid. Kirtland says so outright, so plan on another way to pay.

Check the school first

Federal rules require entry-level training from a provider on the Training Provider Registry, and each campus needs its own listing. If the school isn't listed, the training won't count toward your skills test. Look it up yourself. It takes two minutes.

Who can help pay

Michigan Works!

This is the best first call if you qualify. Michigan Works! Northeast has used federal WIOA money for Alpena's CDL program. Find your agency.

Trucking companies

Some train new hires in exchange for a work commitment. Read the contract and find out what you owe if you leave early.

Workforce Pell, maybe later

Truck driving is on Michigan's eligible list for Workforce Pell, but programs must run at least 8 weeks and many CDL courses are about 3. I found no published list of approved Michigan programs as of November 2026.

The steps at the Secretary of State

  • Bring your license and proof of legal presence and Social Security number to a Secretary of State office.
  • Pass the knowledge and vision tests. You need 80%.
  • Get your learner's permit and hold it at least 14 days.
  • Finish your registered training.
  • Pass the skills test with an approved testing business, then return to pay and get the license.

You also need a federal medical card. Drivers 18 to 20 can only drive inside Michigan. Our truck driver guide has Michigan pay and openings.
