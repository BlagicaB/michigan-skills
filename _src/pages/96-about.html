About Michigan Skills

Built by a Michigan mom who needed it herself.

I'm Blagica Bottigliero. I live in Michigan and I'm the mom of two teenagers. Michigan Skills is the guide I wish I'd had when I started looking at what's out there for them.

Why I built it

Like a lot of parents, I wanted to understand every option my kids have, not just in high school but for their careers and beyond. College credit in high school. Career and technical education. Trade schools. Apprenticeships. Free community college. What it costs, who qualifies and when you have to decide.

The answers were out there, but they were scattered across dozens of websites: state agencies, school districts, colleges, unions and employers. I filled pages of notes. I met with counselors and talked with people at schools, colleges and training programs. Every conversation turned up something new, and almost nobody had the whole picture.

So I put it all in one place. If I needed this, I figured other Michigan families did too, along with adults thinking about a new career and the counselors and teachers who help them.

What this site is

Michigan Skills brings together everything I researched into one guide. It's an aggregation: the information comes from the state agencies, laws, colleges and programs that publish it, and every fact links back to its source. What I add is the part that was missing, which is putting the pieces side by side in plain English so you can see how they fit together.

It's independent. Michigan Skills is not a state agency, school district or college, and it doesn't run any of the programs it describes. Rules change every year, so I date every page and you should always confirm details with the official source before you apply.

Help me make it better

If you know a program I missed, see something out of date or want to suggest an edit, please reach out. Counselors, parents, students, employers and program staff are all welcome. I read everything.

Email hello@michiganskills.com or use this form.

How it stays free

Michigan Skills is free for families and job seekers, always. Over time, organizations that want to reach adults can become clearly labeled partners. Partners never change the order of the options or what the guide says about them, plans for students never show sponsors, and personal information is never sold.
