How to become a lineworker in Michigan

To become a lineworker in Michigan, most people finish a one-year lineworker program at a community college or get into the ALBAT union apprenticeship, then work 3 to 4 years to reach journeyman. You'll need a commercial driver's license and must be comfortable climbing poles. It's one of Michigan's best-paid trades, with a median of $51.39 an hour, but only about 295 openings a year, so it's competitive.

Last checked: October 2026. Pay is the Michigan median from the federal government (May 2025).

Ways to get trained, cheap or free

Community college

Lansing Community College: about 13 months including climbing school, $10,115.50 in-district (2025-26), with a track run with Consumers Energy. Gogebic Community College (Ironwood): 1 year, $9,996 without CDL or $15,004 with. Alpena Community College also offers utility technology. LCC · Gogebic · Alpena

Union apprenticeship

Michigan outside lineworkers apprentice through ALBAT, the American Line Builders training program. Journeyman status generally takes 3 to 4 years, with raises every 6 months (IBEW 876). Application windows are short, so check often.

Get the CDL early

Programs require you to be eligible for a Michigan CDL. How the CDL works
