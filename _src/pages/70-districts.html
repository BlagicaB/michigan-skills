What does my area offer?

Your school district is only half the answer.

Michigan has hundreds of local school districts, plus charter schools. Most of the expensive hands-on programs (welding labs, health science, auto tech, construction) are not run by your local district. They are run by one of 56 intermediate school districts, called an ISD, RESA or ESA, which serves every district in a county or region. So to see your real options, you need to know your ISD.

Three layers, one student

Your local district

Your high school. It sets your schedule, approves dual enrollment and virtual courses and may run some CTE classes of its own.

Your ISD

Usually runs the career tech center, often an Early Middle College and countywide programs. Students typically spend half the school day there and the rest at their home high school.

Your community college

Where dual enrollment classes, Early Middle College credits and many apprenticeship classes actually happen.

Every ISD does it differently. Some run one big tech center (Kent, Ottawa, Ingham). Some split programs across high schools in a consortium (much of Macomb and Wayne). Some partner with a community college for everything. That is why two families 20 miles apart can get completely different answers to the same question.

Look up the exact programs near you

The state keeps a searchable list of every approved CTE program. Here is how to use it.

  1. Open CTE Programs Offered on MI School Data.
  2. In the search box, type your ISD's name, not just your district. That way the tech center programs show up too.
  3. Set Program Type to Secondary CTE. You get a map and a table of every approved program and the building it's taught in.
  4. Have a specific career in mind? Clear the location and pick the program (welding, nursing assistant, IT) to see every school nearby that teaches it.
  5. Then go to the tech center's own website for application windows, bus routes and half-day schedules, because the state list doesn't have those.

For Early Middle College, use the EMC locations map or MDE's 2026-27 list of all approved EMCs (about 190, by county).

Questions to ask your school

Print this or screenshot it. Bring it to a counselor meeting. Every one of these has a real answer, and you are entitled to it.

  • Which ISD do we belong to, and which CTE programs at the tech center can our students attend? Is there transportation?
  • Does our district offer an Early Middle College? If not, can students join the ISD's or a neighboring district's?
  • What is the deadline to apply for dual enrollment next semester, and which colleges do we partner with?
  • How do I sign up for free virtual courses, and what is the deadline?
  • Which AP courses do we offer, and is there help with the exam fee?
  • Do any CTE programs here lead to college credit, an industry certification or a registered apprenticeship?
  • Who at the school helps with work-based learning or internships?
  • Can my student's Educational Development Plan be updated to include these?
