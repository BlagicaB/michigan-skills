Michigan Promise Zones

A Michigan Promise Zone is a city, county or school district that promises local high school graduates a scholarship covering at least the tuition and required fees for an associate degree at a Michigan community college. Michigan has 14 certified Promise Zones. Each sets its own rules, and many also pay toward a bachelor's degree or a trade program.

Last checked: October 2026 against the Michigan Promise Zone Authority Act and the Michigan Department of Treasury.

How do Promise Zones work?

  • Who qualifies: students who live in the zone and graduate from a public or private high school there. A GED counts. Zones can scale the award by how many years a student lived or went to school there, and can set GPA or enrollment rules.
  • The minimum: by law, the promise must cover at least tuition and required fees for an associate degree at a Michigan community college. It can't go higher than the cost of a bachelor's degree at a Michigan public university.
  • Trades count: the law defines the promise to include vocational programs that teach a trade, and many zones pay for certificates and apprenticeship programs.
  • In Michigan only: Promise Zone money can't be used at out-of-state schools.
  • How they're paid for: private donations to start, then half of the growth in the state education tax collected in the zone.
  • Last dollar: students must use federal, state and local grants first, so the Promise pays what's left of tuition and fees.

Sources: MCL 390.1663 · MCL 390.1667 · MCL 390.1677

Can you use a Promise Zone and the Community College Guarantee together?

Yes, and they stack in a set order. For 2026-27 the state applies Pell first, then the Promise Zone scholarship, then the Michigan Achievement Scholarship or the Community College Guarantee to fill what's left. Pell-eligible community college students also get the $1,000 Achievement bonus. Since every program covers tuition, ask your college's financial aid office how your package adds up, and whether leftover Promise or Pell money can go toward books.
