Free CNA Training in Michigan: Ways to Get Paid Back or Paid to Train

By Blagica, Michigan mom of two teenagers · November 8, 2026

Search for free CNA classes in Michigan and you'll mostly find junk. The real answer is a state and federal rule most people never hear about.

Nursing homes may have to pay you back

If a Medicaid-certified nursing facility hires you within 12 months of your test, it must reimburse your training and testing costs. Part-time and per diem jobs count.

  • The state cap is $1,263 as of Oct. 1, 2026, or your actual cost if lower. A facility can choose to pay more.
  • Give form MSA-1326 to your employer, not the state.
  • The facility has six months from your hire date to pay.

Keep your receipts. Leave before six months and you lose the reimbursement. Hospitals, hospice and home care employers don't have to pay it.

Four ways to pay little or nothing

Train at a nursing facility

A facility can't bill you for its own training, can't take it out of your wages and can't require you to stay beyond six months as a condition of reimbursement. Under federal rules, if you have a job or job offer on your first day of class, you can't be charged. Some employers advertise this, like Corewell Pine Ridge, which says it trains non-certified aides. Call facilities near you and ask.

Take a class and get reimbursed

Pay for a program up front, then use the rule above when you're hired.

Ask Michigan Works!

If you qualify, Michigan Works! can pay for short training. West Central Michigan Works! names CNA as an example.

Start in high school

Michigan has no age requirement for training and testing, and many career tech health programs include it. Ask your county tech center.

Read before you sign

Before you accept a "free" class, ask what you owe if you leave, how long you must stay and who pays for the exam. The CNA guide covers pay, the 75 hours of training and how to get on the state registry.
