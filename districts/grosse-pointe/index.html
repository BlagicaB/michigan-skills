Grosse Pointe: what's here, what's near, what's next.

Grosse Pointe Public Schools (about 6,400 students, two high schools: North and South) is known for strong academics: 27 AP courses and most graduates headed to four-year colleges. This snapshot looks at the other doors: college credit, career and technical education, the trades and hands-on exploration. It shows what the district runs, what a Grosse Pointe student can reach nearby and where families should ask questions.

Offered here: the highlights

  • Cybersecurity is a standout. A three-level sequence at North with Cisco and CompTIA certifications, the kind employers hire on. Level I needs teacher and counselor recommendations.
  • Every culinary student earns ServSafe, the food safety credential restaurants require.
  • South's design track is deep. Up to eight levels of Engineering Graphics and six of Architecture, plus CAD and Small Engine Repair. These feed the senior Trade and Industry co-op.
  • Seniors can work for credit. Co-ops run about 15 hours a week, and a co-op student can carry up to 5 credits.
  • Team 1189 has competed at the state championship. Robotics is one of the best ways for a tinkerer to see engineering, machining and electrical work up close.
  • The 2025 bond (passed with 70%) includes new career and technical education spaces at North and South.

Sources: GPPSS High School Program of Studies 2026-27 · GPPSS CTE · MI School Data, CTE Programs Offered (Sept. 2026) · South HS Profile 2025-26 · GPPSS 2025 bond · The Blue Alliance, Team 1189

Reachable nearby

Henry Ford Early College (Dearborn)

An Early Middle College run by Dearborn Public Schools with Henry Ford College and Henry Ford Health. Open to Wayne County residents entering 9th grade who pass reading and math tests. About 50 seats, with a lottery if more apply. Applications run December to February, so current 8th graders apply this winter.

Henry Ford Early College

Dual enrollment colleges

  • Macomb Community College, South Campus in Warren
  • Wayne County Community College District, Eastern Campus on Conner
  • Henry Ford College, Dearborn
  • Wayne State University and Oakland University

Early College of Macomb is limited to Macomb County students, so Grosse Pointe residents don't qualify.

Trades training for after high school

  • IBEW Local 58 electrical apprenticeship, Warren. Ages 17+, diploma or GED and a year of algebra. Next window posted spring 2027.
  • Plumbers, pipefitters, carpenters and more: see union training centers.

Summer and after school

Henry Ford College career camps, Wayne State and Lawrence Tech engineering camps, Michigan Tech's statewide program, DAPCEP Saturday classes and more. Build a plan and they're matched to your child's grade and interests.

Questions to ask

Bring these to a counselor meeting or the CTE coordinator. Each one has an answer that isn't published yet.

  • Which colleges do Grosse Pointe students dual enroll at, what are the deadlines and how much does the district pay per course?
  • Are shared-time seats in the Macomb consortiums (building trades, machining, nursing, EMT) still available for 2026-27? Is there any transportation help?
  • State data lists 3 CTE programs at South and 8 at North. Can South students take North-only programs like cybersecurity?
  • How does the district's 2-online-credits-per-year practice line up with the state's 2-courses-per-term rule?
  • Which employers and hospitals host co-op and medical shadowing students?
  • Are there college credit agreements beyond the one with Macomb, for example for culinary, TV production or marketing?
  • Is the district considering an Early Middle College, building trades or a youth apprenticeship partnership, especially with the new CTE spaces in the bond?
  • Will the Skilled Trades Career Night continue, and is it open to South and middle school families?

Who to ask: the high school counseling offices (North 313-432-3200, South 313-432-3535), the district CTE coordinator, Transition Services at North for outside CTE placements and the Curriculum office (313-432-3042).

For district leaders

A strong foundation with room to grow

Grosse Pointe already has the hard parts: certification-based CTE, a deep design sequence, senior co-ops, a nationally competitive robotics team, family demand (a trades night now in its third year) and voter-approved money for new CTE spaces. Within reach:

  • Publish the dual enrollment partners and deadlines. It costs nothing and it's the most common parent question.
  • Join or build an Early Middle College pathway with Henry Ford College, Macomb or Wayne County Community College District. The district keeps receiving per-student funding for the fifth year.
  • Add a youth apprenticeship link with a nearby union training center, starting from the Trade and Industry co-op students who already work in the field.
  • Name the employer partners behind co-ops and shadowing, and invite more through CTE advisory committees.
